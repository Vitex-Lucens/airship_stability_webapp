Heuristic Design Approach

This code implements a heuristic design approach for airships.

A primary challenge in airship dynamics is that elliptical hull forms are fundamentally dynamically unstable. This presents an inverse problem compared to heavier than air aircraft: whereas conventional aircraft are typically statically unstable but dynamically stable, airships are statically stable yet dynamically unstable.

Practically, this means an airship cannot maintain straight-line flight, it will always be in a turn. Consequently, the design must ensure this turn is stable without continuous control input. To achieve this, the fins must be sized and positioned to effectively counteract the turning moment generated by the hull's lift.

Using the work of Walter Rudolph Munk and Harold Gordon Lamb, this code defines the necessary fin size and position to neutralize the body's turning force. It also includes an option to adjust ballast distribution to tune the vessel's static stability margin. Additionally, a panel-method solver is included for basic aerodynamic analysis of the hull and fin cross-sections.

Why Parameterised Design

Parameterised design enables rapid exploration of the design space. Instead of constructing a single geometry and evaluating it, a handful of inputs drive the full airship definition: hull length and radius, nose and tail extensions, fin span and chord, and the operating environment. This makes it possible to develop intuition about how each parameter influences buoyancy, mass, stability, and handling before committing to detailed modelling.

The method is intended to produce an initial classic airship design that is capable of stable flight. It is not a full flight-dynamics simulation, but a fast, physically consistent first pass that can guide later analysis.

Key Concepts

Coordinate Frame

All quantities are expressed in a right-handed body-fixed frame with x directed forward along the longitudinal axis, y to starboard, and z downward. This convention is maintained consistently across the geometry, mass, aerodynamics, and inertia models.

Hull Form

The hull is modelled as an axisymmetric body of revolution formed by three contiguous segments: an elliptical nose, a cylindrical centre body, and an elliptical tail. The nose and tail semi-axes govern the fore-and-aft fineness of the hull.

The centre of volume (CV) is evaluated by direct volume integration over the hull point cloud, excluding fin contributions. The centre of gravity (CG) is determined from the distributed mass of the structure, fins, lifting gas, and any ballast.

Fins and Static-Dynamic Stability

Ellipsoidal bodies moving through a fluid at incidence generate a couple that tends to rotate the body into the flow. The tail surfaces are therefore sized to produce a counter-moment sufficient to hold the airship in a steady turn without sustained control input. This equilibrium condition is central to the heuristic fin-sizing method.

Ballast

Ballast repositions the CG along the x-axis, thereby adjusting the longitudinal static margin. The required ballast mass follows from the moment balance between CG, CV, and the total vehicle weight.

Buoyancy

Net lift is governed by the density difference between the displaced ambient air and the lifting gas. Atmospheric properties are obtained from an ISA-based model with altitude and temperature inputs; helium and hydrogen are provided as gas options.

Inertia

Rigid-body mass moments are computed by integrating discrete component masses about the CG. Virtual, or added, mass is treated through Lamb's coefficients for an ellipsoid, evaluated numerically for the actual hull fineness ratio rather than interpolated from tabulated values.

Panel Aerodynamics

A two-dimensional vortex-panel method solves for the incompressible potential flow about the hull meridional section and an isolated fin section. The boundary is discretised into straight panels carrying piecewise-constant vorticity; the no-penetration condition yields a linear system for the panel strengths, from which Cl, Cd, and surface pressure coefficient Cp are obtained.
